How it works

Why weight and stiffness set the period

Think of a loaded bus on its springs: the heavier it is, the more slowly it bounces. Think of a diving board: the stiffer it is, the faster it springs back. A building behaves the same way, and one simple formula captures both effects.

T = 2π √(m / k)

With mass in tonnes and stiffness in kN/m, the answer comes out in seconds. Quadruple the mass and the period doubles, because of the square root.

Resonance: matching the push to the swing

Pushing a child on a swing works best when each push is timed to the swing’s own rhythm. In Steady mode, set the shaking period equal to the building period and the sway keeps building up. Damping is what finally limits it, so try lowering damping to 0 and see the difference.

Why near-fault pulses matter

Ground motions close to a fault often contain one strong velocity pulse with a definite period. In Pulse mode, fire a single-cycle pulse and compare a pulse period close to the building period with one much shorter or longer. This ratio of pulse period to structure period is a key idea when comparing near-fault and far-fault records for fragility analysis.

What the model leaves out

This is a single-degree-of-freedom model: one lumped mass on one spring with viscous damping, staying elastic. Real structures have many modes, cracking, yielding and soil interaction. It is a starting point for the physics, not a design tool.
